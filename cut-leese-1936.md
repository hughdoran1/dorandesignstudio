## What happened to it

Theobald did get out, and the route is documented, though not the route anyone would guess.

Jacobs went on to write the *Jewish Encyclopedia* article on William of Norwich. It carries Theobald, the convert, the lot, in the same words. Its bibliography cites his own review, and cites Jessopp and James.

In 1938 Arnold Leese, founder of the Imperial Fascist League, published *My Irrelevant Defence: Meditations Inside Gaol and Out on Jewish Ritual Murder*. It has a chapter on the English cases, and a bibliography of works supporting the blood accusation. Jessopp is not in it. James is not in it. Thomas of Monmouth is not in it. Cambridge is not in it.[^leese]

His Theobald paragraph appears twice, on page 12 and on page 39, and both times it is uncited. The page 39 version follows the *Jewish Encyclopedia* almost word for word. He cites that encyclopedia eighteen times.

He had the thread three times over and never pulled it. Strack's refutation, which he attacks eleven times for what it leaves out, carries the citation in a footnote on page 177. The *Catholic Encyclopedia* carries it. The *Jewish Encyclopedia* carries it. He took the story from all three and the source from none.

And he sends his readers to look at a painted rood screen at Loddon in Norfolk, which is the frontispiece of the book he never opened.

## 1936

M.R. James died at Eton on 12 June 1936.

Twenty two days later, on 4 July, *The Fascist* ran "Jewish Ritual Murder." On 15 July a member asked the Attorney General in the Commons whether he proposed to prosecute. The answer was that the matter was under consideration.[^hansard] On 21 September, a hundred and one days after James died, Leese was sentenced at the Old Bailey to six months.

And the court would not have heard James anyway. The charge was effecting a public mischief, and under the law of libel the truth of the accusation was no defence. When Leese asked the only witness against him, Inspector Kitchener, whether he had been under the impression that ritual murder was a thing of the past, and the man said yes, the judge intervened.

> "The truth of a libel is no defence, I must point out again."[^judge]

Forty years of work on whether it was true, and an English courtroom ruled that the question did not arise. James's demolition was not rejected. It was not reached.
