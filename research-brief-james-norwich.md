# Research brief: M.R. James, William of Norwich, and the escape of the blood libel

For an agent with working web access. The originating session had all outbound
network blocked and could use search-tool summaries only, never opening a page.

## What this is for
A short essay on Mark Fisher's December 2007 Wire interview with Burial, in which
Burial raises M.R. James's "Oh, Whistle, and I'll Come to You, My Lad." The
argument: James's scholarly day job produced the 1896 Cambridge edition of Thomas
of Monmouth's "Life and Miracles of St William of Norwich," the founding document
of the blood libel; "Oh, Whistle" is a parable about unearthing buried things; and
something got out.

## Scope limit, non-negotiable
The essay is about TRANSMISSION, not about the killings. Do not offer or research
any view on who killed William of Norwich in 1144 or Hugh of Lincoln in 1255. Do
not relitigate either case. Out of scope.

## Already established, do not re-verify
- Interview: Mark Fisher, The Wire issue 286, December 2007.
- Manuscript: CUL Add. 3037, from the Brent Eleigh (Suffolk) parish library
  bequeathed c.1700 by Edward Colman of Trinity; bought by Cambridge 1889; James
  identified it as the unique copy of Thomas of Monmouth.
- Edition: Jessopp and James, Cambridge, 1896 (some sources say 1897). Jessopp
  wrote Introduction chapters I to IV, James chapters V to VII, Preface joint.
- Key quotes already transcribed from the 1896 text (Preface "starting-point";
  Jessopp's "has not yet spent itself"; James's "the evidence for a ritual murder
  is simply nil"; Theobald / Narbonne, Book II ch. xi; "child of unusual
  innocence", Book I ch. iv).
- Joseph Jacobs reviewed it: "St. William of Norwich," Jewish Quarterly Review
  9.4 (July 1897): 748-755, JSTOR 1450801.
- Arnold Leese, Imperial Fascist League: ritual murder claims in The Fascist,
  July 1936; convicted of public mischief; six months, refused the fine;
  "My Irrelevant Defence" published April 1938.
- **Leese's bibliography (pp. 56-57) does NOT list Jessopp or James.** His sources
  are Acta Sanctorum, Butler's Lives of the Saints, Annales Ecclesiastici,
  Rohrbacher; Rohling, Drumont, Gougenot des Mousseaux, Desportes, Monniot;
  Utikal, Bischoff, Der Sturmer (May 1934); Burton (1898); Walsh (1931); and for
  England, William Prynne, "A Short Demurrer to the Jewes", 1656.

## PRIORITY 2: the transmission route that actually exists

  archive.org/details/bim_early-english-books-1641-1700_a-short-demurrer-to-the-_prynne-william_1656

- Cross-check: does James discuss the Bollandists or the Acta Sanctorum in his
  chapter VII on the cult? (Grep the 1896 djvu text for "Bollandist".)
